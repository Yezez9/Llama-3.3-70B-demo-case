# Dataset: 120 Years of Olympic History — Athletes and Results

**Source:** Kaggle — 120 Years of Olympic History: Athletes and Results
**Category:** Sports & Athletics — Olympic Games Medals (1896–present, dataset stops at Rio 2016)
**Size:** 271,116 rows x 15 columns (each row = one athlete's participation in a single Olympic event)
**Columns:** ID, Name, Sex, Age, Height, Weight, Team, NOC, Games, Year, Season, City, Sport, Event, Medal

## Key Findings

**Finding 1 — Top countries by total medal count:**
The USA leads all-time Olympic medal counts with over 2,460 gold medals, well ahead of Russia (~1,220) and Germany (over 1,000). However, the gap between top countries has narrowed since the 1990s as more nations invest in sports programs.

**Finding 2 — Medal growth over time:**
Total medals awarded per Games have grown steadily over the 120-year span, mainly because new sports and events keep getting added. A visible dip appears in 1994, the year the Winter and Summer Olympics stopped being held in the same calendar year.

**Finding 3 — Medal distribution by sport:**
Medal distribution is concentrated in a few sports: Athletics, Swimming, and Gymnastics rank in the top five by total medals, with Rowing as a less-expected fourth entry.

**Finding 4 — Age distribution and individual dominance:**
Olympic medalist age peaks sharply at 24, with most medals concentrated between ages 22–26. Individual achievement follows this pattern, highlighted by Michael Phelps (USA), who captured a dataset-record 28 total medals across four Olympic Games between ages 19–31.

## Overall Story
Olympic success has historically concentrated in a small group of wealthy, well-resourced nations, but participation and competitiveness have broadened significantly over the last century. A handful of exceptional athletes can dominate medal tallies, and the structure of the Games itself (which sports are included, when Winter/Summer are held) shapes medal counts as much as athletic performance does.

## Limitations
- Dataset stops at Rio 2016 — excludes Tokyo 2020, Beijing 2022, Paris 2024; any "present-day" ranking is outdated.
- Early Olympics (1896–1920s) had far fewer participating nations and almost no female athletes, inflating the apparent dominance of a few countries.
- Team events count every member of a winning team as a separate medal-winning row, distorting individual-athlete comparisons.
- National boundary changes (e.g. USSR → Russia, East/West Germany → Germany) complicate long-term country-level comparisons without careful NOC-to-country mapping.
